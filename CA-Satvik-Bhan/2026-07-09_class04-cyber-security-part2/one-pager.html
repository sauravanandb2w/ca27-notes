Class 4 — One-Pager: Cyber Security Part 2 (Impact · Challenges · Writing)

1. Impact ladder

  • Individual → business → nation → international. Cyber heads: Economic · Social · Political · National security · Geopolitical.
  • Internal-security topic: weight economic, national security, geopolitical above social/political.
  • Attack question → no digital arrest / scam-hub examples (those are crimes). Use malware, ransomware, DDoS, supply chain.

2. Economic

  • Individual: loss of savings (digital arrest, scam hub, extortion, fake loans).
  • Business: loss for MSMEs/corporates (boss/CEO scam); higher cost of doing business — audit, fraud monitoring, customer redressal, cyber insurance, compliance, training.
  • BFSI: weakens financial integrity; co-op banks = systemic risk; hits financial inclusion (UPI/QR), JAM.
  • Nation: budget for CERT-In, MHA forensics, I4C, NATGRID, Pratibimb; social-sector spend may shrink.

3. Social & political

  • Social: trauma, humiliation, anxiety, stigma → family harm (isolation, suicide) → digital hesitation (elderly, disabled, rural; SC: right to digital access, Art. 21) → women/children (morphing, CSEM, Blue Whale) → youth (instant gratification; money mules).
  • Political: e-governance/service delivery hit (hospitals, municipal, certificates, welfare portals) → loss of trust & credibility → disinformation, dissent → non-state actors (NE groups, Manipur). High social capital (Nordics) resists fake news.
Rule: don't write only your own suffering. Be a sponge: size the answer to the question.

4. National security & geopolitical

  • National security: non-state actors · extremist groups · hostile intelligence networks; critical infra (hospitals, power, banking, telecom, transport/ports). JNPT ransomware 2017; GPS spoofing (West Asia shipping; aircraft near western border/J&K).
  • Geopolitical: dependence on Global North for toolkits, software, capacity (AI triples speed, scale, low entry); dependence on bilateral/multilateral cooperation (India–Myanmar on cyber slavery: evidence sharing, financial tracing, infra takedown, asset recovery; Israel); loss of credibility of India's DPI model and Global South leadership.
  • PYQ 2025: India–Africa digital partnership (mutual respect, co-development, long-term institutional) vs China's debt model, US's exploitative model; IIT Madras Zanzibar, Kenya, UPI.
  • Conclusion word: strategic impact.

5. Challenges (map)

  • 1 Volume: CERT-In ~13.9 → 15.9 → 20.4 lakh (2022–24): better reporting + higher vulnerability.
  • 2 Digitalisation: 1.4 bn Aadhaar; 86% households online.
  • 3 Evolving crime: digital arrest, scam compounds, FaaS (WormGPT, FraudGPT).
  • 4 Geography: hostile neighbours = cyber frontiers (APT36 Transparent Tribe; Ladakh grid; Srinagar army school) · proximity to Myanmar/Thailand/Laos (KK Park) · weak border-area resilience (asymmetric) · vast maritime geography (~11,000 km coast; ~17 submarine cables, 14 landing stations: Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Thoothukudi, Thiruvananthapuram) · concentration of CI in metros (data centres, exchanges, fintech).
  • 5 Policy: 2013 policy outdated · no maritime cyber policy · fragmented coordination (CERT-In, I4C, NCRB, DoT/TRAI, NSCS, NCIIPC, RBI/SEBI, state police) · no unified command (Israel has) · sectoral gaps.
  • 6 Federal: police/public order = State List; weak state cyber-policing; jurisdiction; borderless crime vs unequal capability.

6. "Other" challenges

  • Public–private trust deficit: private-run CI, under-reporting (reputation, penalties, litigation) → weak threat-intel sharing.
  • Privacy–security dilemma: surveillance apps, mandatory installs (Sanchar Saathi, DoT), large-scale data collection.
  • Geopolitical: Global North dependence; export/access limits (US on Mythos); limited global cooperation.
  • Tech/resource: R&D ~0.66% of GDP; no sovereign Mythos-class model.
  • HR: forensic experts, malware analysts, privacy engineers, AI-security researchers, cyber prosecutors.
  • National security: BFSI, ports + submarine cables, smart grids, industrial systems.
  • AI-led: authenticity (deepfakes), scale, attribution, trust (doctored police videos), frontier-model threats (Mythos).
  • Citizen: low awareness, poor hygiene.

7. Answer writing

  • 1 Underline keywords → 2 structure around them (show asked heading) → 3 intro 15–30 + body ~200 + conclusion.
  • Q: Impact of cross-border cyber attacks + challenges (15 marks, 250 words). Intro: name borders (China, Pakistan, Myanmar; proximity to Thailand, Laos) — don't define cyber attack; polygon of securities. Impact ~100 words (no psychological trauma). Challenges: digitalisation, policy/federal, geography, HR, tech/resource, AI (Pak–China, Qihoo 360).
  • Conclusion: new cyber policy + international collaboration; UN comprehensive convention on terrorism; call out proxy terror and cyber terrorism together.
  • Way forward: solve the issues or expand steps already taken.
  • Q1: AI is a double-edged sword — discuss AI-led cyber threats for India.
Verify: incident counts, R&D %, cable counts, SC right to digital access, Israel pact dates, Pratibimb. Pending: steps taken, AI challenges, way forward (next class).
