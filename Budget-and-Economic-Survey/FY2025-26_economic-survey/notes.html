How to use this note

The Survey has 17 chapters. The instructor covers what is relevant for both Prelims and Mains: a few facts are Prelims-critical (flagged PT), while most of the data, terms and state best practices are for Mains answer-writing.

Recurring theme across chapters: shift from access/quantity to quality/outcomes — in education, health, jobs, financial inclusion and skilling.

Ch 1 — State of the economy

Global context

  • Fragmentation of the global economic order from geopolitical competition; scepticism toward free trade and multilateral bodies like the WTO.
  • Trade disruptions — tariffs, sanctions, export controls, reciprocal tariffs → trade wars.
  • Diverging growth: US growth driven by AI; Europe mixed; China facing deflation (weak domestic demand, property crisis, hit to its export-led model); Japan moderate growth with inflation and rate hikes.
  • Advanced economies ran high primary deficits after the pandemic; India and Brazil are the notable exceptions that brought deficits down PT.

Why India stands out (fiscal prudence)

  • Shrinking fiscal deficit (FY27 target 4.3%); better quality of spending (capex); plugging leakages via DBT; strong direct/indirect tax and non-tax revenue (RBI surplus).

Economic statecraft

Economic policy vs. economic statecraft: policy uses fiscal/monetary tools for domestic growth and stability; statecraft deliberately uses economic tools (tariffs, export controls, sanctions, strategic fiscal policy like China's BRI) for foreign-policy and security goals.
  • Interdependence, once seen as efficiency and stability, is now seen as a channel of vulnerability → deglobalisation, friend-shoring, near-shoring.
  • India should move beyond resilience to strategic indispensability — offering goods and services partners can't ignore or substitute.

Domestic economy — demand side

Real GDP growth estimate: ~7.4%.
Private final consumption expenditure: ~61.5% of nominal GDP — the primary anchor (tax rationalisation, low inflation).
Gross fixed capital formation: ~30% of nominal GDP (supported by public capex).
Government final consumption expenditure: ~9.9% of nominal GDP.
Exports: ~21.5% of nominal GDP (the Survey gives exports, not net exports).

Supply side

  • Services — primary driver, ~9.1% growth.
  • Industry — growth accelerator, ~6.2%, led by manufacturing (plus construction).
  • Agriculture — stabiliser, ~3.1%; crops volatile, but allied sectors (livestock, fisheries) grow steadily PT.

Statistics and potential growth

  • Nowcasting — predicting the present, near future and recent past using high-frequency indicators (UPI transactions, e-way bills, electricity/cement/steel output, air and rail traffic), because official data comes with a 2–3 month lag.
  • Cobb-Douglas framework for potential output; total factor productivity (efficiency gains from better use of both capital and labour) averaged ~1.9%, driven by digital technology, allocative efficiency (ease of doing business, IBC) and human capital.

Poverty

World Bank raised the international poverty line from $2.15 to $3 (June 2025).
India: extreme poverty ~5.3%; lower-middle-income poverty line ~23.9%.
Drivers: inclusive growth, targeted welfare, social-sector investment.

Statistical reforms (remember names) PT

  • Annual Survey of Incorporated Service Sector Enterprises (complements ASUSE for unincorporated firms).
  • All India Debt and Investment Survey (from July 2026 — what households own and owe).
  • Situation Assessment Survey of Agricultural Households (2026-27).
  • Household Income Survey.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • First Advance Estimates: real GDP 7.4%, GVA 7.3% in FY26 — fastest-growing major economy for the 4th straight year. FY25 was 6.5%.
  • FY27 projection 6.8–7.2%; medium-term potential growth ~7%.
  • PFCE grew 7.0% → 61.5% of GDP, highest since 2011-12; GFCF grew 7.8%, steady at ~30%.
  • Manufacturing GVA +7.72% (Q1) and +9.13% (Q2) FY26; services 9.1% (FAE); agriculture 3.1%.
  • Credit-rating upgrades: S&P BBB- → BBB; CareEdge Global assigned BBB+.
  • Tendulkar-line poverty: 21.9% (2011-12) → 4.7% (2022-23) → 2.3% (2023-24). NITI MPI: 55.3% (2005-06) → 11.28% (2022-23).
  • Social-protection coverage 22% (2016) → 64.3% (2025).
  • 29 central labour laws merged into 4 Labour Codes (notified 21 Nov 2025); CPI series to be rebased.

Ch 2 — Fiscal developments

  • New fiscal anchor: from FRBM's fiscal-deficit target to a debt-to-GDP target of 50% ± 1% by FY31 PT.
  • Direct-tax dominance — ~58.8% of gross tax revenue (FY25); buoyancy above nominal GDP. The income-tax NUDGE framework shifted from coercive enforcement to non-intrusive, data-led nudges (behavioural economics) for voluntary compliance.
  • GST 2.0 — two rates (standard 18%, merit 5%) plus a demerit rate of 40%; inverted duty structure fixed.
  • Disinvestment: the Companies Act requires 51% government stake for a "government company". The Survey proposes amending this to 26% for listed companies (enough to block special resolutions) to enable monetisation while retaining control — not yet amended PT.

Tech-driven public finance

  • From the old "credit push" model (bulk advances → idle cash, diversion, interest cost on borrowed money) to a "debit pull", just-in-time model.
  • SNA-SPARSH for centrally sponsored schemes; Treasury Single Account for central sector schemes — funds drawn only when spent. Benefits: less diversion, less idle cash, interest savings.
  • Route optimisation of PDS — most efficient routes from warehouses to FPS: ~₹250 crore annual savings; CO₂ cut up to ~35%; better inter-state movement; less pilferage.

State finances

States' combined fiscal deficit ~3.2% of GDP (FY25).
States with revenue surplus fell from 19 (FY19) to 11 (FY25).
Committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest, subsidies) >60% of revenue receipts.
  • Unconditional cash transfers (often to women): fiscal strain, rigidity (no sunset clause or review), crowd out productive investment, and don't reliably improve human-capital outcomes like nutrition and education.
  • Better: conditional transfers with review — Mexico's Progresa, Brazil's Bolsa Família, the Philippines' 4Ps (Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino), USA's Opportunity NYC.
  • Market failure in State Development Loans: interest rates don't differentiate by fiscal health because of concentrated ownership (banks holding to maturity, regional preferences), low secondary-market liquidity and poor disclosure of off-budget liabilities (15th FC urged better disclosure) PT.
  • MITRA study (1980-81 to 2022-23): higher transfer volume doesn't automatically raise growth — what matters is quality of spending, fiscal discipline and above all capital expenditure.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Centre's fiscal deficit: 4.8% (FY25) → 4.4% BE (FY26); revenue deficit 0.8%, lowest since FY09.
  • Debt-to-GDP 55.7% (FY25), down 7.1 pp since 2020; anchor 50±1% by FY31. Return to rule-based regime (debt ~50%, deficit ~3%) may be considered after 2031 (PRS).
  • Revenue receipts 8.5% of GDP (FY16-20 avg) → 9.1% (FY22-25) → 9.2% (FY25 PA).
  • Direct-tax share of gross tax 58.2% in FY25 (51.9% pre-COVID). ITRs 6.9 crore (FY22) → 9.2 crore (FY25).
  • NUDGE = Non-intrusive Usage of Data to Guide and Enable.
  • Effective capex 2.7% of GDP (pre-pandemic) → ~4% (FY25). States' capex ~2.4% of GDP; SASCI (50-year interest-free loans) uptake ₹4,49,845 crore over 5 years.
  • Subsidies 1.9% (FY22) → 1.1% of GDP (FY26); revenue expenditure 13.6% → 10.9% of GDP.
  • GST gross ₹17.4 lakh crore Apr-Dec 2025 (+6.7%); taxpayers 60 lakh (2017) → 1.5 crore+.

Ch 3 — Monetary management & financial intermediation

  • Low inflation allowed repo cuts to ~5.25%; stance moved to neutral; CRR cut to 3%.
  • Reserve money (M0) growth fell — not tightening, but because the CRR cut reduced banks' deposits with RBI. Banks lent more, so broad money (M3) grew ~12.1% and the money multiplier rose to ~6.21 (Dec 2025) PT.
  • Banks: GNPA and NNPA at multi-decade lows; capital adequacy ~17.2%; stress now in agriculture and unsecured personal loans rather than large corporates.
  • RRB consolidation — "one state, one RRB": from 196 down to 28; record profits; GNPA down to ~5.4%.
  • RBI FREE-AI framework (Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI) with seven sutras: trust, people first, innovation over restraint, fairness and equity, accountability, understandable by design, safety/resilience/sustainability.
  • Financial inclusion: from opening accounts to credit utilisation. Microfinance contracted ~14% due to stress — inaccurate income assessment, poor visibility of borrowers' other debts, rigid pricing, intense competition. Graduated-credit models (PM SVANidhi, MUDRA) work.
  • RBI Financial Inclusion Index: access 35%, usage 45%, quality 20%.
  • Capital markets: domestic institutional investors now own more Indian equity by value than FPIs — a shock absorber PT.
  • Insurance: FDI limit raised to 100%; insurance density rose but penetration stagnated PT.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Repo cut 125 bps since Feb 2025 (to 5.25%). Liquidity: CRR cut ₹2.5 lakh crore, OMOs ₹6.95 lakh crore, forex swaps ~$25 bn.
  • Lending rates (WALR) fell 59 bps on fresh and 69 bps on outstanding loans (Feb-Nov 2025).
  • GNPA 2.2% (Sep 2025), multi-decade low; slippage ratio 0.7%; NPA recovery rate 13.2% (FY18) → 26.2% (FY25).
  • RBI regulation-making framework (May 2025) + Regulatory Review Cell (from 1 Oct 2025) reviews each regulation every 5–7 years.
  • RRBs: net profit ₹7.6 thousand crore (FY24), ₹6.8 thousand crore (FY25).
  • PSB Credit Assessment Model uses digital footprints for MSME loans (linked to CGTMSE).
  • RBI FI-Index 67.0 (Mar 2025) vs 64.2. PMJDY 55.02 crore accounts (36.63 crore rural/semi-urban). Account ownership 35% (2011) → 89% (2021).
  • Microfinance: 95% women and 80% rural borrowers; portfolio ₹33,517 crore (FY14) → ₹2,38,198 crore (FY25).
  • IBC: ₹3.99 lakh crore realised in 1,300 cases (170% of liquidation value); S&P moved India's insolvency regime from Group C to B.
  • Unique investors cross 12 crore (Sep 2025), ~25% women; financial-sector assets ~187% of GDP (CY24).

Ch 4 — External sector

Geostrategic globalisation

From seamless, efficiency-driven globalisation (comparative advantage, WTO) to geostrategic globalisation — still interconnected, but choosing whom to trade with and depend on, prioritising resilience and national security. Signs: new alignments (BRICS+, IMEC), friend-shoring (politically aligned partners), near-shoring (geographically close partners); trade-policy and economic-policy uncertainty indices peaked in 2025; weakening trade agreements, aggressive industrial policy, competition for critical minerals.

India's performance

Total exports: record ~$825 billion (FY25).
Share of global merchandise exports ~1.8%; of commercial services ~4.3%.
Non-petroleum, non-gems-and-jewellery exports growing.
Agriculture paradox: 2nd-largest agricultural producer by value but only ~2.2% of global agri exports — ad hoc export bans hurt reliability.
Services exports: record ~$387.5 billion; current account deficit ~0.8% of GDP.
Remittances: ~$135.4 billion (FY25) — world's largest recipient; USA has overtaken the Gulf as top source (~27.7%) PT; remittances now exceed gross FDI inflows.
FDI: top destination in South Asia; 4th globally for greenfield projects; risk-adjusted FDI return ~7.3% (2nd after Indonesia).

Economic complexity PT

  • Economic Complexity Index (Harvard Growth Lab) — productive knowledge, measured by diversity of exports and their ubiquity (how many other countries make them). India ranks 44th of 145 (2023), unchanged since 2019 — exports are low/mid-complexity (petroleum, gems, rice, medicines).
  • Complexity Outlook Index — how easily a country can diversify into complex products. India ranks 2nd — strong foundational know-how. The gap between present and potential calls for targeted policy (PLI), technology and scaling up manufacturing.

Outward FDI

  • Not just "capital flight": gains via the capital channel (repatriated profits), knowledge channel (practices, technology, standards) and resource/market channel (foreign assets, brands, GVC integration) — if the home economy has absorptive capacity (skills, infrastructure, R&D, institutions).
  • OFDI rose from ~$14.4 billion (FY24) to ~$23.6 billion (FY25), increasingly asset- and capability-seeking.
  • Investment Development Path (John Dunning): net outward investment position moves from negative (investment importer) to positive (exporter) as an economy matures. India is at stage 3 — still a net recipient but investing abroad more.

Exchange rate

A weaker rupee: the trade-channel gains (exports, import substitution) outweigh the financial-channel costs (capital outflow). No need to keep the rupee artificially strong to please foreign investors.

Net international investment position PT

  • Germany overtook Japan as the world's largest net creditor (2024); China also a top creditor.
  • USA is the largest net debtor; India is a net debtor, ~11th largest.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Total exports $825.3 bn (FY25, +6.1%): merchandise $437.7 bn, services $387.6 bn (+13.6%); services surplus a record $188.8 bn.
  • Share of world merchandise exports 1% (2005) → 1.8% (2024); commercial services 2% → 4.3%. India is the 7th-largest services exporter.
  • UNCTAD TDR 2025: 3rd in the Global South for trade-partner diversification.
  • Agri exports $34.5 bn (FY20) → $51.1 bn (FY25); pharma exports $1.9 bn (FY01) → $30.5 bn (FY25).
  • CAD 0.8% of GDP in H1 FY26 (1.3% in H1 FY25). FDI inflows $81 bn in 2025 (+13%).
  • Forex reserves $701.4 bn (16 Jan 2026) = ~11 months of imports, ~94% of external debt.
  • Trade deals: India–UK CETA, India–Oman CEPA, New Zealand FTA (Dec 2025); India–EU FTA concluded Jan 2026, awaiting ratification.
  • Remittances $135.4 bn (FY25), largest in the world; rising immigration controls flagged as a risk.

Ch 5 — Inflation

  • Globally, inflation has subsided — advanced economies ~2–3%, emerging ~5%. China in deflation; Japan the outlier — ended negative rates; wage-driven inflation from labour shortages (ageing).
  • India's CPI averaged ~1.7% in FY26 (to December), driven by a crash in food prices (vegetables, spices) — food inflation went negative.
  • Momentum vs. base effect: momentum = month-on-month change; base effect = influence of last year's level on year-on-year inflation. FY26's low inflation was largely a base effect (high base in FY25) PT.
  • Structural excess demand in pulses and edible oils (consumption exceeds production) → counter-cyclical tariffs: raise duties when world prices crash (protect farmers), cut them when prices surge (protect consumers).
  • Rural inflation fell below urban because food has a larger weight in the rural basket.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • CPI averaged 1.7% (Apr-Dec 2025), the lowest in the CPI series. Oct 2025 = 0.3%, the lowest reading of CPI (2012=100).
  • Food and fuel = 52.7% of the CPI basket; food inflation negative since June 2025.
  • Core inflation rose 3.8% → 4.62% (Dec 2025), driven by gold and silver; excluding precious metals, core is easing.
  • Potato, onion, tomato and garlic prices fell 20–40%; the edible-oil duty cut eased prices from Aug 2025.
  • Global inflation 8.7% (2022) → 4.2% (2025). FY27 outlook: gradual rise within the 2–6% band; risks are base metals, gold/silver and rupee depreciation.

Ch 6 — Agriculture

Agriculture and allied: ~1/5 of national income, ~46% of the workforce; decadal growth ~4.45%.
Crops ~3.5% vs. livestock ~7.1% and fisheries ~8.8% — allied sectors drive growth.
Horticulture ~33% of agri GVA; output ~362 million tonnes — exceeds foodgrains PT.
India: largest producer of dry onions (~25% of world); 2nd in vegetables, fruits, potatoes.
Gross irrigated area ~55.8% of gross cropped area — but ~67% of rice is irrigated vs. <15% of millets and ~26% of pulses.
Feed/fodder >70% of milk production cost; fodder crops only ~4.6% of gross cropped area.
Ideal NPK ratio 4:2:1; actual skewed to ~10.9:4.1:1 by urea overuse.
Ethanol blending saved ~₹1.4 lakh crore in forex.

Reform ideas

  • Fertiliser: urea is artificially cheap → overuse → soil damage. Let prices reflect cost and pay farmers the subsidy as per-acre DBT (by land, crop and agro-climatic zone) — nudging balanced use.
  • Energy vs. food security: higher prices for maize-based ethanol are pulling land from pulses and oilseeds.
  • Procurement: open-ended procurement of rice and wheat exceeds needs; cut it and pass the fiscal savings to farmers as per-acre incentives to diversify.
  • PMFBY tech: YES-TECH (yield estimation using remote sensing) and WINDS (weather information network with automatic weather stations and rain gauges).

State best practices (for Mains)

  • Andhra Pradesh — drone-based resurvey and digital land titles; e-farm market linking farmers to traders through Rythu Bharosa Kendras.
  • Bihar — Chaur Vikas Yojana repurposing low-lying land.
  • Madhya Pradesh — Sampada/"Sarthi"-type digital MSP purchase platform (name unclear in captions).
  • Assam — state irrigation plan. Uttar Pradesh — groundwater rules. Karnataka — FRUITS farmer registry. Jharkhand — GIS-based climate-smart agriculture.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Decadal growth 4.45% (FY16-FY25), the highest of any decade: livestock 7.1%, fisheries 8.8%, crops 3.5%. Agriculture grew 4.6% in FY25.
  • Foodgrains a record 3,577.3 LMT (AY 2024-25), +254.3 LMT.
  • Horticulture ~33% of agri GVA; output 362.08 MT, ahead of foodgrains (other tables show 367.72 MT for 2024-25: fruits 114.51, vegetables 219.67).
  • Livestock GVA +195% (FY15-FY24); fish production +140% (2014-24).
  • PM-KISAN: over ₹4.09 lakh crore released since inception.
  • Priorities (PRS): productivity and resource sustainability, R&D, climate resilience, private investment in storage and processing, and high-growth areas (horticulture, dairy, poultry, fisheries).

Ch 7 — Services

  • Precocious, services-led growth — India shifted to services at a much lower per-capita income than others.
  • Services ~53.6% of GVA; high growth, low volatility; services exports keep expanding even as goods stagnate globally.
  • Services' share of FDI rose from ~77.7% (FY16-20 average) to ~80.2% (FY23-25), with ~89% concentrated in five segments: information and communication (largest), professional services (GCCs), finance and insurance, energy, trade.

NITI Aayog decadal assessment

  • Employment–output paradox: services employ ~30% of workers with better job quality (~51% regular wage vs. 25% in industry, 1% in agriculture), but high-end services create output, not many jobs; jobs come from low-value sub-sectors (trade, hospitality, transport).
  • Regional concentration — Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Maharashtra ~40% of services output; Bihar is also service-heavy (~58%) but low-value.
  • Urban phenomenon — ~60% of urban workers vs. ~20% rural; ~60% of urban women vs. ~10.5% of rural women. In ICT, urban women sometimes out-earn men.

Do services exports build state capacity? No — four reasons

  1. They are firm-selective, not system-forcing — they succeed on skills and talent even when infrastructure and institutions are weak.
  2. They exit without voice — move quietly between cities rather than pressing the state to fix systems (unlike manufacturers).
  3. They impose no hard constraints on the state — low employment elasticity, so shocks stay local.
  4. They are skill-based, not system-based. Manufacturing disciplines the state; services don't.

Other points

  • Servicification of manufacturing — services (R&D, design, software, logistics) embedded in manufacturing; ~17.7% of manufactured export value (2020); EVs as "software on wheels".
  • GCCs — ~1,700 units employing ~19 lakh; from routine support to core operations and innovation.
  • Data-centre paradox — India generates ~20% of world data but hosts ~3% of data centres; capacity plan 1.4 GW (2025) → 8 GW (2030) is energy-intensive. Survey: classify data centres as a distinct category (not commercial buildings), give green-power access, tax clarity and visas, and publish anonymised public data.
  • Medical tourism; marinas for the blue economy; the orange (creative) economy; port turnaround cut from ~43 to ~30 hours.
  • Adopt Singapore's Research, Innovation and Enterprise plans; India's step: Anusandhan National Research Foundation.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Services ~54% of GDP (H1 FY26), growing 9% vs 7% a year earlier.
  • Services export growth 7.6% (FY16-20) → 14% (FY23-25); ~8% in FY26 (Apr-Nov). Software >40% of services exports; consulting share 10.5% → 18.3%.
  • Services employment elasticity 0.43 (2011-24) → 0.63 post-COVID, second only to construction. Services = 61.9% of urban jobs and 51.7% of EPFO net additions.
  • Tourism 5.22% of GDP (FY24), 8.46 crore jobs; forex earnings $35 bn (2024).
  • IT-ITeS revenue $283 bn (FY25); 1,700+ GCCs employing ~19 lakh.
  • Data centres ~150 of the world's ~11,000; GenAI start-ups ~240 → 890+ (H1 CY24 → H1 CY25).
  • Space: ~2% of the global market; 393 foreign satellites launched for 34 countries (2015-24). Survey suggests a national marina policy and hiking trails.

Ch 8 — Industry

Industrial GVA growth ~7% (H1 FY26).
Medium- and high-tech activities ~46.3% of manufacturing value added.
UNIDO Competitive Industrial Performance rank: 37th (2023), up from 40th PT.
Steel: 2nd-largest crude steel producer, yet a net importer in FY26 (cheap imports).
Coal: record output FY25; 5th-largest reserves; 2nd-largest consumer; ~55% of energy mix.
Cement: 2nd-largest producer; per-capita use ~290 kg vs. world ~540 kg.
Electronics: 7th-largest export category (FY22) → 3rd (FY25).
Pharma: 3rd largest by volume; ~20% of global generics.
Textiles: 6th-largest exporter; over-reliant on cotton as the world moves to man-made fibres.
National Manufacturing Mission target: manufacturing to 25% of GDP by 2035.
R&D: 3rd in scholarly publications; Global Innovation Index 38th (2025); GERD stuck ~0.64% of GDP (China ~2.4%, US ~3.5%), with low private share (advanced economies >75% private).
  • Corporates diversifying from bank credit to commercial paper and corporate bonds — deepening the bond market.
  • Quality control orders — helped toys turn from net import to net export, but blanket QCOs on intermediates and raw materials without domestic substitutes hurt export competitiveness.
  • MSME online dispute resolution scheme — MSMEs avoid filing delayed-payment cases against large buyers for fear of losing business; ODR encourages amicable settlement before formal action.
  • Backward GVC participation (import inputs, assemble, export) suits a labour-rich economy — the BVAX ratio (foreign value added in exports). Low BVAX looks "self-reliant" but means weak GVC integration (cf. Vietnam, China).

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Coal output 1,047.52 MT (FY25). Mobile production ₹18,000 crore (FY15) → ₹5.45 lakh crore (FY25), ~30x.

  • GII rank 66 (2019) → 38 (2025), 1st in Central and Southern Asia. Business share of R&D 41% (China 77%). RDI Fund ₹1 lakh crore over 6 years.
  • DPIIT start-ups ~500 (2016) → 2 lakh+ (2025). India ranks 6th in patent filings and 4th in trademarks (2024).

Ch 9 — Investment & infrastructure

Central capex up ~89%: ~₹5.92 lakh crore (FY22) → ~₹11.21 lakh crore (FY26).
Infrastructure spending multiplier: 2.5–3.5× GDP PT.
  • Silos to integration: fragmented planning raised logistics and transaction costs and hurt competitiveness → PM GatiShakti National Master Plan (GIS-based) and the National Logistics Policy's ULIP (Unified Logistics Interface Platform) integrating FASTag, customs, rail, Vahan data.
  • Financing: from bank loans to InvITs, AIFs and the like for cheaper long-term money.
  • RBI Project Finance Directions 2025 — limits indefinite extension of the "date of commencement of commercial operations" to stop evergreening of stressed project loans.
  • Small and Medium REITs — SEBI cut the minimum asset size from ₹500 crore to ₹50 crore, democratising the channel for mid-sized developers PT.
  • PPP reset — government as a true partner taking early-stage risks (land, clearances), with renegotiation for unforeseen events; viability gap funding 40% (economic) and 50% (social projects).

Sector data

National highways up ~60% (FY14–FY25); high-speed corridors ~10×; ~8,000 km of roads for PVTG habitations (PM-JANMAN).
Railways ~99% electrified; Kavach rollout; dedicated freight corridors.
Aviation: 3rd-largest domestic market; airports 74 (2014) → 164 (2025); new Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam PT.
Ports: turnaround ~30 hours; 32 national waterways operational; Jal Marg Vikas on NW-1.
Power: installed capacity ~509.74 GW (+11.6%); discoms posted a positive profit after tax in FY25 for the first time.
Renewables ~49.83% of installed capacity (~253.96 GW): solar ~52%, wind ~21%, large hydro ~20%, bio ~5%, small hydro ~2%. India 3rd in renewables and solar, 4th in wind PT.
Teledensity ~86.76%; internet connections ~25 crore (2014) → ~101.78 crore (2025); broadband ~6 crore → ~99 crore.
100% FDI via automatic route in less-sensitive space segments; Jal Jeevan Mission ~81% rural tap water.
  • India Energy Stack — DPI for power: from centralised to decentralised, consumer-driven markets; peer-to-peer trading; households and MSMEs become prosumers.
  • Telecom's four pillars: Samavesh (inclusive), Viksit, Tvarit (accelerated), Surakshit (secure).

  • World Bank: India is among the top 5 LMICs for private investment in infrastructure and takes 90%+ of South Asia's.

Ch 10 — Climate, environment & energy transition

  • Sea-level rise: ~95% of sites globally show no statistically significant acceleration; where acceleration exists it is often local or non-climatic. Not climate denial — a call for evidence-based adaptation and institutional prudence over alarmism.
  • Scarce fiscal resources in poor countries should prioritise poverty reduction and human capital over near-term climate milestones.
  • India's per-capita emissions ~2.9 t vs. world ~6.7 t; domestic climate finance rose from ~3.7% of GDP (FY16) to ~5.6% (FY22); ~98% of climate finance domestically sourced.
  • MISHTI — mangrove restoration (carbon sink, coastal buffer).

Sub-national adaptation (Mains examples)

  • Meghalaya — Mega Rice project (water security). Odisha — Pani Panchayats (community water governance). Tamil Nadu — coastal restoration and blue-economy project.
  • Ahmedabad — South Asia's first Heat Action Plan (2013) and parametric heat insurance compensating informal women workers when temperatures cross a threshold.
  • Jodhpur — community cooling stations combining solar power and traditional cooling.
  • Uttarakhand — community radio for early warnings.

Mitigation and minerals

  • India 4th globally in total installed renewable capacity; SHANTI Act (private nuclear participation, graded liability).
  • India needs ~336 GWh of battery storage by 2030; inter-state transmission charges waived for storage.
  • Critical minerals: chokepoints (copper price spikes). Standards dilemma — rich-country ESG and traceability standards act as non-tariff barriers: higher compliance costs, asymmetrical enforcement → entry barriers, a value-chain trap (developing countries stuck mining, not refining), and a sustainability premium raising costs and hurting a just transition. India's response: National Critical Mineral Mission.
  • Climate finance skew — more than half goes to advanced economies, ~30% to China, ~15% to other developing countries. India issues sovereign green bonds; cities issue municipal green bonds.
  • Greenium — the lower yield investors accept on green bonds. Strong in Europe (transparent reporting); modest in India (limited liquidity, held to maturity) PT.
  • From Pigouvian command-and-control to market-based instruments — e.g., Surat's particulate matter emissions trading scheme, the world's first market-based ETS for particulate pollution PT.
  • PARIVESH single-window clearances; colour-coded industry categories (red, orange, green, white, blue); decriminalisation of minor environmental offences (with higher penalties for serious harm); green-belt norms tied to pollution potential.
  • Circular economy — Odisha's waste-to-wealth mission; Kerala's KERA (climate-resilient agri value chains, World Bank).

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • Survey's framing: a development-centred, whole-of-economy strategy — "development is itself a form of adaptation".
  • Non-fossil share 51.93% of installed capacity (Dec 2025), ahead of the NDC's 50% goal.

Ch 13 — Rural development & social progress

Theme: from government-led to community-driven (top-down → bottom-up): people's participation and democratic decentralisation.

Poverty & social mobility

  • Inclusive growth needs social mobility — intra-generational (within a lifetime) and inter-generational (across generations).
  • Monetary vs. non-monetary poverty: income below a threshold vs. simultaneous deprivations across indicators.
  • NITI's national MPI: 3 dimensions (health, education, standard of living), 12 indicators. MPI 55.3% (2005-06) → 14.96% (2019-21) → 11.28% (2022-23); high-incidence states (Bihar, UP) saw sharp falls.
  • Monetary (Tendulkar line): 21.9% (2011-12) → 2.3% (2023-24). World Bank $3 line: extreme poverty 5.3%.

Graduation approach to poverty

Graduation approach: a time-bound, sequential, multi-component package for the ultra-poor — productive asset (e.g., a cow or goat), consumption/financial support, skilling, and hand-holding — so the household "graduates" and stays self-sufficient after support is withdrawn.
  • Why: traditional tools (DBT/subsidies like PDS, wage schemes like MGNREGS, skilling like PMKVY, SHG microfinance) leave many dependent for a decade or more; the ultra-poor struggle to complete skilling or join groups.
  • Models cited: Bihar's Satat Jeevikoparjan Yojana, Kerala's Kudumbashree ultra-poor model; the Union adopted a graduation-based initiative under DAY-NRLM PT.
  • Social-sector spending share rising from FY23 (24.4% → 26.6% of total spending, per graph); social-protection coverage 22% (2016) → 64.3% (2025); education spending CAGR 11%, health 8% (FY22–26); rural improved drinking water 99.6% (cuts women's time poverty).

Rural employment — MGNREGS → VB-G RAM G

  • Person-days under MGNREGS fell over 53% — coinciding with falling rural unemployment, i.e., more alternatives and a reviving rural economy.
  • VB-G RAM G Act 2025: 125 days (up from 100); four priority areas — water security, rural infrastructure, livelihoods, climate and disaster resilience; 60-day agricultural pause; weekly wages (at most within a fortnight); admin ceiling raised 6% → 9% for professional and technical staff — a trap: not everything was cut PT.

Digital & village governance

  • SVAMITVA (drone mapping of rural properties, ownership records); Namo Drone Didi (rural women trained as drone operators).
  • ULPIN / "Bhu-Aadhaar" — a 14-digit unique ID for each land parcel, an "Aadhaar for land", under the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme; ~99% of rural records digitised PT.
  • SabhaSaar — AI tool that auto-generates gram sabha minutes and agendas.
  • Panchayat Advancement Index (MoPR): multi-domain index tracking localised SDGs across 9 themes — poverty-free and enhanced livelihoods, healthy, child-friendly, water-sufficient, clean and green, self-sufficient infrastructure, socially just and secured, good governance, women-friendly. Uses: evidence-based planning, competition among panchayats, community engagement.

Village commons

  • Commons (grazing land, ponds, forests) ≈ 15% of India's area (as stated); degraded land has expanded to ~30% of area.
  • Tragedy of the commons (Garrett Hardin, 1968): shared resources get over-used (over-grazing); his fixes — privatise or impose strict state control.
  • Elinor Ostrom (Nobel 2009) — communities can manage commons sustainably. Her 8 design principles PT:
    1. Clearly defined boundaries (resource and users)
    2. Rules matched to local conditions
    3. Collective-choice (participatory) rule-making
    4. Monitoring by people accountable to users
    5. Graduated sanctions (proportionate)
    6. Low-cost conflict resolution
    7. Recognised right to organise (local autonomy)
    8. Nested enterprises (multiple layers)

Tribal empowerment

  • STs ≈ 8.6% of population (Census 2011; the lecture said 8.9%) in ~1.45 lakh tribal villages; biodiversity-rich areas; traditional ecological knowledge.
  • Infrastructure: PM-JANMAN — 75 PVTGs in 18 States + 1 UT, 100% saturation; DAJGUA (Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan) for tribal-village infrastructure gaps PT.
  • Economic: Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission — Van Dhan Vikas Kendras for minor forest produce; NSTFDC for finance; FRA.
  • Education and capacity: Eklavya Model Residential Schools; Adi Karmayogi Abhiyan.
  • Smart tribal farming — traditional eco-friendly knowledge + weather, soil and market data.
  • Case studies: Odisha — Strengthening Livelihoods of Rural Tribal Farmers (integrated farming + sprinklers); Madhya Pradesh — community seed banks; Jharkhand — solar irrigation co-operatives + mobile advisories.

Social justice & sanitation

  • SHREYAS (higher-education scholarships for SC/OBC); VISVAS (interest subvention for SHGs/entrepreneurs).
  • SMILE — Support for Marginalised Individuals for Livelihood and Enterprise: transgender persons and persons engaged in begging.
  • NAMASTE — National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem: safety, rehabilitation and formalisation of sanitation workers / manual scavengers PT.

Ch 14 — Artificial intelligence

Theme: from the West's top-down, frontier-model concentration to an India-specific bottom-up, frugal AI strategy solving local socio-economic problems.

Labour-market impact

  • McKinsey 2025: 88% of organisations use AI in at least one function.
  • US study of professional, business and information services: (1) no immediate mass displacement, but organisational restructuring; (2) a structural decline in labour intensity of output — slightly fewer workers per unit of output; (3) impact is sector-specific (GenAI-exposed sectors), not yet macro.
  • Frontier AI is concentrated in a few firms and countries (capital, talent, energy) → entry barriers, supply-chain risk, technological dependence. India is capital-, compute- and energy-constrained → pursue a distributed model.

Six policy asymmetries (trade-offs)

Trade-offIndia's choice
Frontier vs. applicationSmall, application-specific models for real needs (farmers' language data, market access, learning)
Scale vs. inclusionLabour-abundant (10 mn+ entrants a year) → calibrated, slow diffusion; augmentation over replacement; sequence AI into augmenting sectors (health, education) first
Open vs. proprietaryMiddle path — "open with stewardship": open models tuned on Indian data, run on national AI infrastructure that sets quality and safety (like UPI); avoids vendor lock-in
Compute/energy vs. resource limitsDistributed, energy-light models
Regulation vs. innovationPacing problem and Collingridge dilemma → soft-touch, risk-based regulation
Strategic autonomy vs. global integrationDomestic solutions plus participation in global innovation networks

Vehicle: the AI-OS initiative — a shared, UPI-like layer for frugal AI.

Human capital

  • "AI displaces tasks, not capabilities." Cognitive pyramid: information processing → retrieval/summarisation → analysis/interpretation → domain expertise/strategy → judgment, synthesis, direction (top = safe).
  • Be a system architect, not a task-master; build "underground" (foundational, structural) knowledge, deep domain expertise and continuous learning.

AI governance

  • Any AI regime balances safety, innovation and inclusion.
  • EU AI Act 2024 — a single omnibus, risk-based law. US — voluntary, sector-specific, light-touch.
  • India: sequencing — coordination (IndiaAI Mission) → capacity (AI Safety Institute, AI Economic Council) → targeted binding rules where risks are clear.
  • AI Economic Council: labour-market sensitivity, public-interest safeguards, ethical non-negotiables (no opaque surveillance or worker monitoring, no "black boxes"), guarding against cognitive atrophy. Plus a data-governance framework balancing global data flows with domestic oversight.

AI safety

  • Endogenous risk (from within) — e.g., sycophancy: models validate what users want to hear rather than what is accurate PT.
  • Exogenous risk (misuse) — AI lowers entry barriers to harm, e.g., weaponisation, bio-terror.
  • Responses: AI Safety Institute; scenario-based testing; red-teaming (trying to break the system).

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • AI raises the productivity of capital relative to labour — a tension for labour-abundant India.
  • India's edge is application-led innovation and human-capital depth, not frontier-model scale. Prioritise decentralised, sector-specific, open and interoperable systems with shared infrastructure.
  • Sequencing: "coordination first, capacity next and binding policy leverage last". Data governance through trusted flows and auditability rather than rigid localisation.
  • Proposed AI Economic Council with five principles: human primacy, labour-market sensitivity by design, sequencing over speed, co-evolution of technology and human capital, public-interest safeguards PT.
  • Examples: Bhashini, AI4Bharat, thermal-imaging breast-cancer screening; "Earn and Learn"; move India from AI "back office" to "front office".

Ch 15 — Urbanisation

Theme: stop treating cities as a welfare burden or chaotic habitats; redefine cities as critical economic infrastructure.

What is "urban"?

  • Foundations: demographic scale (size) and density.
  • Census town criteria: population ≥ 5,000; density ≥ 400/km²; ≥ 75% of male main workers in non-farm work PT.
  • Agglomeration economies: clustering of people and firms raises productivity via (1) deeper labour markets, (2) knowledge spillovers, (3) shared infrastructure. World Bank: doubling city size raises productivity ~12% in India.

Paradox of Indian urbanisation

  • Huge cities without proportionate global influence — congestion instead of productivity.
  • Root causes: weak infrastructure; contradictory policies (low FSI restricting growth); misaligned development (no metro, land reform or affordable housing alongside growth) → outward sprawl.

Statistical illusion & hidden urbanisation

  • Census suggests slowing, top-heavy urbanisation (most urban people in Class-I cities); partly due to ruralisation of industry (manufacturing moving to smaller places).
  • Spatial data reveal hidden urbanisation:
    • UN Degree of Urbanisation (satellite settlement data): ~63% urban in 2015, about double the 2011 Census rate.
    • Night-time lights: small gains in cores, rapid growth at peripheries.
    • Periphery-to-core growth ratio >1 in 16 cities, i.e., peripheries are growing faster than cores.
  • Kerala's dispersed "ribbon" urbanisation: ~82.6% of the population in urban-type settlements (2025), in a continuous strip of homes, shops, schools and hospitals along roads.

Urban governance

"Indian cities suffer because they are administered rather than governed" — they execute orders from above instead of setting their own priorities and solving their own problems. (Likely Mains quote.)
  • Fragmented authority (ULBs, state departments, parastatals; weak mayors).
  • Fiscal weakness: municipal own-source revenue is tiny (well below 1% of GDP, vs. OECD ~2–4%); property tax covers only ~30–40% of potential.
  • New instruments: Urban Challenge Fund (Budget), Urban Infrastructure Development Fund.

Constraints & fixes

  • Land: low FSI → limited vertical growth → horizontal sprawl → costlier land and infrastructure. Urban land becomes "dead capital" (unusable productively) due to restrictive regulations and unclear titles. Fix: digitise titles; reform zoning and FSI together with transit, or density causes congestion collapse.
  • Mobility: National Urban Transport Policy principle — "move people, not vehicles" PT. Mass transit; first- and last-mile links (cycling, shared mobility); transit-oriented development (jobs, housing and mixed use near stations); demand-based parking; congestion pricing (London, Singapore).
  • Water economy: from use-and-dispose to treated used water for non-potable use (industry cooling, construction, landscaping). India treats only ~28% of urban wastewater and reuses ~8% of treated water; ~37% of urban households have sewer connections. The reuse market could be worth ₹2.4–3.2 lakh crore by 2047, with 1 lakh+ jobs. Supported by AMRUT 2.0 (incentives for sewage treatment plants) and SBM-U 2.0 (outcomes, Garbage-Free City star ratings, legacy-waste remediation).
  • Informality (slums, street vendors): an outcome of rapid urbanisation under constrained systems — a governance failure. Yet informal workers are the city's invisible backbone. Shift from eradication to integration: in-situ slum upgrading, recognising vendors (PM SVANidhi).

Social contract & future cities

  • Clean homes but dirty streets is not a cultural trait — it reflects a fragile social contract.
  • Contextual compliance: people behave in the Delhi Metro (clear rules, reliable service, fair enforcement) but not outside. "Civic order is an institutional equilibrium, not a cultural trait."
  • Future cities: treat time as a central resource; streets as social infrastructure ("8-80" principle — streets that work for an 8-year-old and an 80-year-old); creative density (culture, art, food); participatory governance (neighbourhood councils); endowment effect — reliable services lower cognitive load and build psychological ownership of the city.

From the official Survey (PIB, 29 Jan 2026)

  • India is more urban in economic and functional terms than the Census suggests: satellite data (EU GHSL) put India at ~63% urban in 2015, nearly double Census 2011 (31%) PT.
  • World Bank: ~600 mn urban residents (40%) by 2036, generating ~70% of GDP.
  • Constraints: land, housing and mobility supply; fragmented municipal governance; weak city fiscal autonomy.
  • Proposal: every million-plus city to prepare a statutory 20-year City Spatial and Economic Plan (updated every 5 years) with three non-negotiables — transport network plan, housing supply plan with annual targets, and a land-value capture framework.
  • Also: transit-oriented development, congestion pricing, clear land titles and density norms; PM e-Bus Sewa (10,000 e-buses); metro/RRTS ~1,036 km in 24 cities; PMAY-U 122 lakh houses sanctioned.

Glossary

Economic statecraft
Using economic tools for foreign-policy and security goals.
Strategic indispensability
Making a country's goods and services hard for partners to replace.
Nowcasting
Estimating present/near-term activity from high-frequency data.
NUDGE (tax)
Data-led, non-intrusive prompts for voluntary tax compliance.
Credit push vs. debit pull
Bulk advances vs. just-in-time release of funds.
Money multiplier
M3 ÷ M0 (~6.21).
Geostrategic globalisation
Interconnected trade filtered by security and resilience.
ECI vs. COI
Present export complexity vs. ease of diversifying into complex goods.
Investment Development Path
Dunning: net outward FDI rises as an economy matures (India at stage 3).
Base vs. momentum effect
Influence of last year's level vs. month-on-month change.
Counter-cyclical tariff
Raise duties when world prices fall; cut when they rise.
Precocious services-led growth
Shift to services at low per-capita income.
System-forcing vs. firm-selective
Sectors that press the state to improve systems vs. those that bypass them.
Servicification
Services embedded in manufacturing.
Backward GVC participation
Importing inputs to assemble and export.
Prosumer
Consumer who also produces (e.g., rooftop solar).
Greenium
Lower yield investors accept on green bonds.
Standards dilemma
ESG/traceability standards acting as non-tariff barriers.
Expected vs. mean years of schooling
Forward-looking for children vs. backward-looking for adults.
Longevity dividend
Gains from longer, healthier, productive lives.
Pink tax (mobility)
Extra cost women pay for safer transport.
Skill impact bond
Outcome-based financing with an independent evaluator.
Graduation approach
Time-bound, sequential, multi-component exit from ultra-poverty.
Tragedy of the commons
Hardin: shared resources are over-used.
Agglomeration economies
Productivity gains from clustering of people and firms.
Hidden urbanisation
Urban growth in peripheries missed by Census definitions.
Dead capital
Land that cannot be used productively due to rules or unclear titles.
Contextual compliance
Orderly behaviour only where rules, service and enforcement are reliable.
Open with stewardship
Open AI models governed through national infrastructure for quality and safety.
Sycophancy (AI)
Model tells users what they want to hear.
