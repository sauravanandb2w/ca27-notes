1. Feminization of Agriculture

  • Two forms: feminization of agricultural labor (negative — unpaid/underpaid, no land title, "invisibilized") vs feminization of agricultural management (positive — women gain decision-making agency/control).
  • Three farmer-definition models: (1) land-linked/administrative (ownership-based, used for PM-KISAN, excludes tenants/sharecroppers); (2) legislative/activity-linked, narrow mandate (PPV&FR Act 2001); (3) activity-based recognition (National Policy for Farmers 2007, adopted by Maharashtra's 2026 Act).
  • Context: UN's 2026 International Year of the Woman Farmer; Maharashtra Women Farmers Empowerment Act 2026 — first state to grant farmer recognition independent of land ownership via a Woman Farmer Certificate.
  • Data: PLFS — 64% of working women engaged in agriculture; Agriculture Census 2015-16 — women operate only ~14% of landholdings; ~110m women vs ~127m men in agri workforce (2025); >41% of female agri workers unpaid; only ~23% of PM-KISAN beneficiaries are women; 24% productivity gap (women- vs men-managed farms), driven by resource access, not capability.
  • Drivers: male out-migration (construction); agriculture no longer remunerative (distress-driven, not opportunity-driven feminization); social acceptance of farm work vs "domestication" for care work.
  • Structural barriers: patrilineal inheritance → no legal title → financial exclusion (no collateral) → masculinized mechanization → poor market access → triple burden (domestic + care + farm work).
  • Policy responses: MKSP (NRLM) — krishi/pashu/bank sakhis; Namo Drone Didi; Maharashtra's Woman Farmer Certificate. Global: Vietnam's 2003/04 Land Law (joint spousal land titling).
  • Swaminathan's 4 C's: Conservation (seeds/biodiversity), Cultivation (manual labor), Consumption (nutrition decisions), Commercialization (financial control).
  • Way forward: Recognize (activity-linked) → Reduce (women-friendly tech) → Redistribute (joint titling) + gender budgeting.
Must-know: Agriculture is a State subject — activity-linked reform needs state-by-state legislation; Maharashtra is the first mover, others urged to replicate.
Caution: "70% PM-KISAN beneficiaries in Meghalaya" link is to matrilineal inheritance (auto-transcript garbled this as "material inheritance") — verify exact % before citing. Kudumbashree "~44%" figure also unverified.
No land title→Excluded from schemes/credit→Vulnerability→Low productivity
Reform: Activity-linked recognition→Access to credit→More investment→Agency/titles

2. Mayor-in-Council / Urban Governance

  • Context: NITI Aayog report (Apr 2026), "Moving Towards Effective City Government: A Framework for Million-Plus Cities."
  • Constitutional basis: 74th CAA (promised decentralization, under-delivered); Art. 243Q (notifies Nagar Panchayat/Municipal Council/Corporation); Art. 243R (direct election to all municipal seats); Art. 243U (5-year term).
  • Present structure: Council (elected councilors) + Standing committees (mini-ministries) + Municipal Commissioner (state-appointed CEO, real power) + Ward committees. Mayor = ceremonial, 1-2 yr renewable term.
  • Core problem: "bureaucratic centralization" instead of democratic decentralization — only 4 of 18 functions devolved to city governments.
  • Data: 46-47 million-plus cities = ~30% urban population but only ~10% urban area; contribute ~60% of national GDP; own-tax revenue share fell ~43% (FY17) → ~30% (FY23/24); only 162/468 rated ULBs are investment-grade; just ~25 municipal bonds raised ~Rs 3,500 cr (2017-25) vs China's 252 bonds/$142bn in one month (Aug 2025, unverified exact figure).
  • NITI's fix — Mayor-in-Council: directly-elected mayor, tenure co-terminus with council, real executive head (commissioner reports to mayor); cabinet-style governance (portfolio allocation); unified service delivery (parastatals under municipal control); 6 priority sectors: water, transport, sanitation, SWM, urban planning, fire services; timely SFCs + Action Taken Reports.
  • Global models: USA strong-mayor system (New York); East Asia's "residual claimant" model (local body keeps a revenue share, incentivizing growth); Singapore's 1960s master-planning transition.
  • Challenges: state reluctance; inconsistent adoption (Chhattisgarh/MP restored direct election; TN/HP/Rajasthan discontinued); legislative stagnation (failed private member bill); delayed local body elections; "fiscal doom loop" (weak revenue → weak services → taxpayer alienation → weaker revenue).
Must-know: 74th CAA promise vs reality gap = core exam framing. Cities = high demographic + economic weight, but near-zero fiscal autonomy (GST/corporate/income tax all flow to Centre/state).
Case: SC (Aug 2025) directed UP govt to consider converting Noida (no elected local govt) into a Municipal Corporation, after SIT found weak Noida Authority oversight.
Present: Commissioner (state-appointed) = real power→Mayor = ceremonial
Reform: Mayor (directly elected)→Real executive head, cabinet-style

3. Census Towns & Hidden Urbanization

  • Context: Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs' draft report "Census Criteria for Defining Urban Areas" before Parliamentary Standing Committee — argues the 1981 classification criteria are obsolete.
  • Statutory town: notified under law (Art. 243Q) — urban-governed. Census town: meets demographic criteria only, no notification — still rural-governed (Gram Panchayat) despite urban character.
  • 1981 census-town criteria (all three needed): population ≥5,000; ≥75% of male main working population in non-agricultural work (gender-biased — ignores women); density ≥400 persons/sq km.
  • Hidden urbanization: official urban share ~31-32% (2011 Census) vs Economic Survey's DEGURBA-based estimate of ~63% — the gap is unrecognized/"hidden" urban population.
  • Key concepts: DEGURBA (UN's satellite-grid-based global urban classification standard); rural-urban continuum (vs rigid binary) — e.g. Kerala's "ribbon-like" urban conurbation; peri-urban area (metro fringe); sub-urbanization (self-driven small-town growth, e.g. Tiruppur-textiles, Sivakasi-firecrackers); congestion diseconomies.
  • Data: census towns grew from 1,362 (2001) to 3,894 (2011); World Bank — India to be 40% urban by 2036, needs ~$55bn/year urban infra investment.
  • Issues: governance mismatch (urban function, rural governance); fiscal starvation (no urban-scheme access, risk of losing rural-scheme benefits like MGNREGA); political resistance to municipalization (rich avoid tax hike, poor avoid losing rural benefits); outdated one-size-fits-all criteria disadvantage hilly/low-density regions.
  • Case studies: SC (Aug 2025) on Noida; Telangana's CURE Bill/Act 2026 (integrated Hyderabad-Cyberabad-Malkajgiri governance, replaces GHMC Act 1955); Odisha's "build first, notify later" transition model; 16th FC's Rs 10,000 cr peri-urban/urbanization premium; Naksha program (urban land-record digitization, cf. SVAMITVA for rural).
  • Way forward: mandatory Art. 243Q notification once urban criteria are met; state rural-urban transition policies (Odisha model); adopt DEGURBA uniformly; prioritize geospatial land-record digitization.
Must-know: Statutory town = law-based + urban-governed; Census town = stats-based only + rural-governed. This distinction is the crux of every census-town question.
Data point: Census towns nearly tripled in one decade (1,362 → 3,894, 2001-2011) — core evidence of rapid, unrecognized urbanization outpacing statutory classification.
Urban in function/demography→No Art. 243Q notification→Still Gram Panchayat-governed→Governance vacuum
