1. Judicial Appointments — India vs UK vs USA

  • Evolution: First Judges Case (1981, consultation ≠ concurrence) → Second (1993, consultation = concurrence, collegium born) → Third (1998, CJI + 4 senior judges institutionalized)
  • Collegium: judicially evolved, not in Constitution text, all-judge, closed-door; President is final formal appointing authority
  • UK JAC (Constitutional Reforms Act, 2005): 15 members, lay Chair mandatory, public vacancy ads, handles judges + tribunal members both
  • USA: President nominates, Senate Judiciary Committee confirms (televised); lifetime tenure ("good behaviour") → long-term political patronage risk
  • India eligibility (Art. 124(3)): bench route, bar route (10 yrs), distinguished-jurist route (never used — Justice Ujjal Bhuyan urged activating it, Aug 2026)
  • Key cases: CPIO v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal (2019) — CJI office under RTI; Media One (2023) — sealed cover violates natural justice
  • Reform ideas: activate distinguished-jurist route, permanent collegium secretariat, NJAC-style body modeled on NTC (preserving judicial majority)
Must-know quote: Lord Hewart — "justice must not only be done but must also be seen to be done." Use for transparency/conclusion lines.

2. Tribunals & Tribunal Reforms Act 2026

  • Basis: Arts. 323A (administrative) / 323B (other matters — tax, environment), 42nd Amendment 1976
  • Rationale: specialization, speed, cost-effectiveness; problems: vacancy → no quorum → no hearing → pendency
  • Evolution: Sampath Kumar (1987, "effective substitute") → L. Chandra Kumar (1997, only "supplemental," JR is basic structure) → MBA I–V/Rojer Mathew (2010–19, independence tussle) → TR Act 2021 struck down ("legislative override") → TR Act 2026 + NTC
  • NTC: 5 members (Chair = retd SC judge/HC CJ + 2 judicial + 2 technical); judicial majority 3:2 + Chair's casting vote
  • SCSC recommends 1 name + 1 waitlist (was 2 names); 3-month timeline; tenure 5 yrs; retirement 70 (Chair)/67 (members)
  • Gaps: executive veto on final appointment still exists; complaints pre-screened by parent ministry; NTC lacks fiscal autonomy
Must-know data: ~5.36 lakh tribunal cases pending (2025); DRTs highest at ~2.45 lakh; ~Rs 25 lakh crore capital locked across 3.5 lakh+ cases (State of Tribunals report, 2025).

3. PM-AASHA

  • Pradhan Mantri Annadata Aay SanraksHan Abhiyan, launched Sep 2018 — remunerative farmer returns + consumer price stability
  • PSS (procurement via NAFED/NCCF, pulses/oilseeds, capped ~25-30% of production; 100% for urad/masur)
  • PDPS (no procurement — direct MSP-market gap payment, capped at 15% of MSP)
  • MIS (perishables/TOP not under MSP, triggered on >10% price fall, Centre-state loss sharing)
  • PSF (only consumer-centric leg — buffer build/release to cool TOP inflation)
  • Significance: counters cobweb phenomenon, crop diversification (Swaminathan's assured price + assured market), cuts import dependence
  • Challenges: tenant-farmer exclusion (land-record-linked eligibility), only ~2.9-12.4% of pulse output actually procured, trader-farmer collusion risk (cf. MP's Bhavantar Bhugtan Yojana/ICRIER), import-commitment policy incoherence
  • Note: the planned live answer-writing exercise on this topic was deferred, then confirmed dropped/not picked up in Class 9 — treat as independent-practice territory
Must-know: All PM-AASHA legs except PDPS need physical procurement/storage — this is the single best anchor line for any "challenges" answer.
