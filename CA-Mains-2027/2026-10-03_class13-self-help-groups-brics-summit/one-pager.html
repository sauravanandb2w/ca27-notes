1. Self-Help Groups & PRI-SHG Convergence

  • Core SHG features: self-governed (one member, one vote); peer-controlled (basis of "social collateral" — substitutes for land title as bank collateral); small (10-20 members, ensures accountability); informal (no legal registration — flexible, but legally weak); socioeconomically homogeneous (prevents elite capture).
  • NABARD's Panch Sutra (5 principles): regular meetings; regular savings; regular inter-loaning; timely repayment; up-to-date bookkeeping.
  • Conceptual toolkit: development = Stiglitz's 4 capitals (material/natural/human/social, not GDP alone); social capital (organic trust/networks) vs social engineering (top-down state-driven design) vs social business (self-sustaining, non-dividend, profit reinvested in social cause).
  • Empowerment framework: power to (individual agency) · power with (collective bargaining vs state/market) · power within (self-worth, gender consciousness — the cognitive dimension).
  • Evolution: 1980s NABARD poverty-alleviation initiative → SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (microfinance) → SGSY self-employment strategy → NRLM/DAY-NRLM → present shift toward poverty eradication via the "graduation approach" (durable skill/asset-building so there's no relapse).
  • Data: DAY-NRLM mobilized ~10 crore households into ~92 lakh SHGs; Economic Survey — 1.2 crore SHGs, 88% all-women, >96% loan repayment.
  • Challenges: gendered/low-margin vocations; elite capture; high MFI interest rates; weak bookkeeping/no Panch Sutra adherence; Allahabad HC (2020) quashed UP's SHG-preference order for ration shops — held SHGs a "weak legal entity" for accountability.
  • PRI-SHG convergence: collaborative governance — PRIs do policy/sanctioning/funding; SHGs prepare Village Poverty Reduction Plans, mobilize community, implement, and conduct social audit; VPRP integrated into Gram Panchayat Development Plan. Needs untied funds + dedicated professionals.
  • Models: Kudumbashree's 3-tier structure — NHG (neighbourhood) → ADS (ward) → CDS (Panchayat, can register/run social business); JEEViKA Bihar (~1.5 cr households, 1m+ SHGs, 99% repayment); Jharkhand's Birsa Harit Gram Yojana (state+SHG+civil society+private convergence).
  • Emerging finance: Social Stock Exchange (zero-coupon, zero-principal donation bonds); Social Impact Bonds ("pay for success" — investor funds service-delivery entity, independent auditor verifies outcome, govt pays only on success).
Must-know: local-level trust deficit (election compulsions + bureaucratic centralization + 3F problem + weak accountability) drives further centralization — PRI-SHG convergence is the antidote, rebuilding trust-based, participatory governance.
Caution: lecture's "49% income increase" (NRLM evaluation) could not be matched to a precise published figure — independent studies instead show ~9.5-19% gains. Cite cautiously.
Vicious: Gender inequality→No agency→Intergenerational poverty
Virtuous: Thrift & social collateral→Institutional credit→Livelihood & agency

2. BRICS — New Delhi Declaration 2026

  • Context: 18th BRICS Summit, New Delhi, September 2026 (India's chairship) — adopted the New Delhi Declaration; securing consensus among a structurally heterogeneous bloc = a diplomatic success for India.
  • Data: ~49.5% of world population; ~40% of global GDP (PPP); ~25% of global trade; NDB cumulative ~139 projects/~$39bn (India: 35 projects/~$10bn — figures vary by source/date).
  • Five structural debates: (1) non-western reform (India/Brazil/SA) vs anti-western revisionism (China/Russia); (2) hyperexpansion vs institutional cohesion (shift to minilateralism); (3) Washington Consensus (market-led) vs Beijing Consensus (state-led, hub-and-spoke); (4) pyramid of privilege (West = rule-maker, Global South = rule-taker) vs platform of partnership (flat, all rule-shapers); (5) structural heterogeneity/bilateral frictions + energy-cartelization risk (Russia-Saudi-Iran).
  • Financial architecture — "Bretton Woods twins vs Pretoria twins": World Bank↔NDB (long-term development finance); IMF↔CRA (short-term crisis liquidity). Same stated goals, different founding promises.
  • NDB promise vs reality: equal 20% voting share for founders (delivered) vs local-currency financing (failed — ~half of bonds still dollar-denominated) vs shielding members from sanctions (failed — froze Russia operations) vs countering western rating agencies (failed — still dependent on them) vs matching World Bank scale (failed — $39bn vs World Bank's ~$100bn/year).
  • CRA promise vs reality: no-conditionality emergency liquidity (failed — unconditional access capped at 30% of quota, rest needs IMF conditionalities) — mechanism effectively unused in a decade (even South Africa's crisis bypassed it); lacks independent surveillance/monitoring capacity.
  • De-dollarization: dollar remains dominant (market-determined, convertible, robust US system); India resists full de-dollarization (fears yuan dominance, China's hub-and-spoke "project export" model); BRICS tones down de-dollarization language to avoid US secondary-sanctions risk.
  • New Delhi Declaration highlights: UNSC reform; Bretton Woods quota reform; local-currency settlement; strengthen NDB/CRA; WTO reform; reject unilateral sanctions; zero tolerance on terrorism; two-state solution; new India-led agro-inputs/genetic-resources/information-network initiative; CBDR reaffirmed; safe DPI & AI governance.
  • Way forward: minilateral coalitions; AMRO-style (ASEAN+3) macroeconomic surveillance for CRA; raise intra-BRICS trade; dismantle non-tariff barriers; supply-chain resilience.
Must-know: "to what extent can NDB/CRA replace World Bank/IMF" = evaluate promise-vs-reality for each institution separately, not a blanket yes/no.
Caution: "42% of global agricultural output" and exact NDB project-count/dollar figures vary by source/date — flagged as approximate, verify before citing precisely.
