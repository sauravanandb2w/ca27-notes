1. Article 371K & asymmetric federalism

  • Context: MHA proposes a sui generis Art. 371K (Part XXI) for the UT of Ladakh; in-principle consensus with the Apex Body Leh and the Kargil Democratic Alliance. No draft yet (Sep 2026).
  • Dimensions of federalism (DY Chandrachud): origin in the GoI Act 1935 plus a centralised "indestructible Union" after Partition; asymmetric (371–371J, Fifth/Sixth Schedules), balancing (rigid plus flexible amendment) and dialogic (GST Council); identity federalism (culture, Art. 29–30, 371) vs efficiency federalism (73rd/74th Amendments); pre-Bommai centripetal era (India Cement 1990, overruled by MADA 2024) vs post-Bommai centrifugal era (federalism is basic structure).
  • Integration debate: homogeneity (identical Centre–state ties; exceptions breed sub-nationalism and "soft sedition"; Art. 370 abrogation, UCC) vs constitutional pluralism (asymmetry is the "glue"; integration is ongoing negotiation).
  • Amphibian federalism: State of Rajasthan v. UoI (1977) — federal in normal times, unitary in emergencies.
  • Framework: Art. 239 (UT run by the President through an administrator), 239A (UT legislature: Puducherry, J&K), 239AA (Delhi), 240. Art. 371 objectives: culture and customary law; land rights; regional backwardness; peace accords.
  • Ladakh timeline: pre-2019 — 4 MLAs, LAHDCs (Leh 1995, Kargil 2003), 370/35A protections → 2019 UT without a legislature under the LG (democratic deficit) → demands for statehood and the Sixth Schedule → 2024 five new districts.
  • Why not the Sixth Schedule: meant for districts within states (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram; 10 ADCs), not a whole UT; China/Pakistan border sensitivity.
  • 371K model: directly elected UT body with powers over land, culture/language, environment and natural resources; hill councils continue; PRIs at the base.
  • Concerns: security; overlapping jurisdictions; unprecedented use of a state provision for a UT (basic structure); symbolic autonomy (cf. Manipur's 371C Hill Areas Committee); plural demography.
  • Way forward: entrench subjects explicitly (as in 239AA); demarcate powers; continue dialogue — locals are the "eyes and ears" of border security.
Must-know: asymmetric federalism is not a concession to regionalism but the glue of unity in diversity; 371K is a compromise between the Sixth Schedule demand and security.
Add-on to Class 12: James C. Scott, Seeing Like a State — "the state's drive for legibility can exclude those it cannot read" (women farmers without land titles).

2. Blue economy & fisheries

  • Context: Sapta Dhara (15 Aug 2026) — manufacturing, agri and food processing, tech and innovation, Gati Shakti, defence, green and blue economy, soft power. Samudra Manthan offshore-exploration scheme (₹84,084 crore, 60 deepwater wells, Cabinet 31 Jul 2026).
  • Terms: blue economy (economic, social and ecological pillars, plus India's security pillar); blue revolution (productivity plus biosecurity); blue-green revolution (productivity plus sustainability).
  • Data: coastline 11,098 km; 2nd-largest producer; 197.75 lakh t (2024-25); seafood exports ₹62,408 crore / $7.45 bn (shrimp ~69%, US ~36%); ~28 million direct jobs; small-scale fishers ~90% of the workforce and ~10% of catch; women ~44%; 10–15 lost cyclone days a year.
  • Significance: protein and nutrition; jobs and women's empowerment; exports and forex; mangroves and corals as bio-shields; deep-sea critical minerals; net security provider.
  • Challenges: over-exploitation cycle (bottom trawling, by-catch); informality and the low-income trap; 20–30% post-harvest loss; ~10% of agri credit; WPA/CRZ constraints; cyclones and pollution; US tariff exposure; competition from Ecuador and Vietnam; fish-seed shortage (aquaculture ~75% of output).
  • Cases: Odisha's Marine Spatial Plan (Apr 2026, first state, NCCR plus Norway); New Zealand's Māori aquaculture co-governance.
  • Schemes: PMMSY (central sector plus centrally sponsored); PM-MKSSY (formalisation, work-based digital ID); mariculture; catch-to-customer / shore-to-door; RAS.
  • Way forward: traditional knowledge plus satellites; eco-labelling (Lakshadweep pole-and-line tuna); market diversification; reservoir clusters; community co-management and benefit-sharing.
Must-know: use two diagrams — a coastline map and the low-income-trap flowchart.
Caution: the 44% women, 90/10 small-scale, $2.2 bn pollution and 10% credit figures are lecture claims, not verified.
