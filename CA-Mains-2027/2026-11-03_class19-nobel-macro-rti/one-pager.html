1. Macro stabilisation — 2026 Nobel

  • Laureates: Robert E. Hall (Stanford) and Michael Woodford (Columbia); citation: "microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation".
  • Classical: invisible hand; markets self-correct; collapsed with the Great Depression (1929).
  • Keynesian: loss of animal spirits → demand management; investment multiplier; Phillips curve; broke down in 1970s oil shocks (stagflation).
  • Hall (demand side): forward-looking households; random walk of consumption (only surprises move spending); hand-to-mouth households → targeted transfers.
  • Woodford (supply side): sticky prices and wages; menu costs; forward-looking pricing; forward guidance restores confidence.
  • Result: monetary policy is the primary anchor; fiscal policy targeted and predictable.
Must-know: Keynes = demand shock via animal spirits; New Keynesian = stickiness + expectations; India's anchor = inflation targeting (4% ± 2%).

2. RTI Act 2005 — tool and amendments

  • Basis: Art. 19(1)(a) and Art. 21; Raj Narain (1975), S.P. Gupta (1981).
  • Key sections: s.2(h) public authority; s.4 proactive disclosure; s.8 exemptions (public-interest override for personal information, until 2023); PIO; first and second appeals.
  • Time limits: 30 days; 48 hours where life or liberty is involved.
  • 2019 amendment: Centre prescribes information commissioners' tenure (rules: 3 years) and salary (rules: ₹2.5 lakh, per lecture); state commissions brought under Centre's control.
  • 2023 amendment (DPDP Act s.44(3)): personal information fully exempt; public-interest override removed.
  • Challenges: ~3.85 lakh pending appeals (30 Jun 2026, 28 commissions); vacancies; weak suo motu disclosure; few penalties; whistleblower risk.
  • Way forward: operationalise Whistle Blowers Protection Act; public records office; RTI overriding DPDP; political-party disclosure.
Caution: the DPDP s.44(3) change and the 2019 salary and tenure figures are from the lecture; verify the gazette text before quoting.
