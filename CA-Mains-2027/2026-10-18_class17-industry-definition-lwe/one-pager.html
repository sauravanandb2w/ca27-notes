1. Definition of "industry"

  • Triple test (Bangalore Water Supply, 1978): systematic activity + employer–worker cooperation + goods/services. Profit irrelevant.
  • Why it matters: "industry" workers get protection (layoff, strike rights). Labour is on the Concurrent List.
  • Basis: labour–capital asymmetry (Ambedkar: "dictatorship of the private employer"); Art. 23 (forced labour, incl. below minimum wage — Asiad case), Art. 42, 43, 43A.
  • Trade-off: economic efficiency (flexibility, ease of doing business) vs. social protection.
  • Law: IDA 1947 (repealed) → IR Code 2020 s.2(p) builds the triple test into text; excludes sovereign functions, charity, domestic service.
  • Phases: I ambiguity → II worker-welfare era (1978) → III liberalisation (Jai Bir Singh, 2005, referred to larger bench) → IV 2026 nine-judge verdict (20 Aug 2026): triple test for pending cases; IR Code text for future cases.
  • Challenges: interpretive divergence; "manager" vs. "worker" in IT services; gig workers as "partners"; erosion of safety nets; multi-tier litigation.
  • Way forward: harmonised state rules; clarity on "worker"; universal social-protection floor; faster dispute resolution.
Caution: vote count of the 2026 verdict differs across sources (5:4 vs 4:4) — check the judgment.

2. Left-wing extremism (post-2026)

  • Terms: LWE (umbrella) → Naxalism (Maoist-inspired; Naxalbari 1967) → Maoism (rural protracted people's war). Leninism is urban/vanguard-party-based.
  • Protracted people's war: strategic defence ("hide and grow") → stalemate (guerrilla) → offence (cities). Movements can regress.
  • Frameworks: greed vs. grievance (grievance starts it; extortion and levies sustain it); strong societies, weak states (Migdal) → governance vacuum; Lederach's conflict transformation (personal, relational, structural, cultural; pyramid structure).
  • Phases: Telangana (1940s) → Naxalbari (1967) → People's War Group → unified CPI (Maoist), PLGA, Janatana Sarkars (peak) → five-pronged counter-offensive with Operation Kagar (2024–26).
  • Status: India declared free from armed Naxalism (Lok Sabha, Mar 2026); PM reiterated in 15 Aug 2026 speech. Peak: ~20 states, 223 districts (2009–10). Now: a handful of districts.
  • Reasons: security (forward operating bases, CoBRA, CHB); infrastructure (roads, bank branches); rights and governance (FRA, PESA, surrender-rehab policy).
  • Residual risks: regrouping, urban "overground" workers, transnational organised crime, development-induced displacement, social integration of surrendered cadres.
  • Way forward: prevent grievance; keep the strong state (2015 policy, SAMADHAN); conflict transformation; Red Corridor → Development Corridor.
Must-know: the answer on "security vs. infrastructure" needs governance as the third factor.
