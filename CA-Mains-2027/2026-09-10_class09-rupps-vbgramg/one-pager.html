Topic 1 — RUPPsRegistered Unrecognised Political Parties

  • Basis: Art 19(1)(c) (form associations) → Sec 29A, RPA 1951 governs registration (30-day window, public notice in 2 national + 2 local dailies).
  • Pathway: new party → RUPP (registered, unrecognized) → recognized party only on meeting a vote/seat threshold.
  • Shared benefit despite the gap: both recognized parties and RUPPs get tax exemption on donations (IT Act 2025, Sec 12) — this is the core misuse vector.
  • The vacuum: ECI can register but not deregister — only 3 SC-recognized grounds (fraud / unconstitutional constitution-amendment / declared unlawful). Delisting ≠ deregistration (admin-only, reversible).
  • Two loopholes named for any answer: Sec 29C's Rs 20,000 disclosure threshold; IT Act's Rs 2,000 cash-donation threshold.
  • Mechanism: donor gives tax-exempt donation → RUPP books it as "expenditure" → returns cash (minus a cut) → laundered; or "placing & layering" via many sub-Rs-20,000 donations.
  • Reform suggestions: statutory deregistration power (Law Commission 255th Report; ECI 2016 Memorandum); tie tax exemption to a minimum vote-share; ECI-CBDT-ED-FIU data integration; lower/digitize donation thresholds; bring parties under RTI (CIC already ruled they are "public authorities").
Must-know case INC (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare (2002): ECI has no general deregistration power — only fraud, constitution amended in breach of Sec 29A(5) allegiance undertaking, or party declared unlawful.
Must-know data 6 Gujarat RUPPs got ~Rs 1,700 cr donations (FY23-24), > combined non-BJP national parties; only ~750/2,800+ RUPPs fielded candidates in 2024 LS polls; only ~26% file public financial reports (ADR 2025).

Topic 2 — VB-G RAM GReplaces MGNREGA, effective 1 July 2026

  • Constitutional basis: Art 41 & 43 (DPSP), Entry 23 Concurrent List, Art 21 livelihood angle (Olga Tellis, 1985). Right to work = DPSP, still not a fundamental right.
  • Why replaced: poverty down (29%→11.2%, NITI Aayog MPI), rising migration/remittances, gig economy, rising asset ownership, cash transfers — rural landscape restructured.
  • Objectives: wage employment/income security + durable-asset creation (new emphasis).
  • 125 days (vs 100), explicit lean-season scheme — up to 60 peak-season days excluded, workers expected into open farm-labour market.
  • 4 thematic areas: water security, rural infra, livelihood infra, disaster mitigation.
  • Normative (formula-based) allocation replaces demand-driven, open-ended funding — fixes the old "Matthew effect" (capacity-rich states over-captured funds) but sacrifices real-time responsiveness.
  • Work within 15 days; unemployment allowance retained, disentitlement-for-non-presentation clause removed; expanded biometric/geotagging digital layer.
  • Gram Rozgar Guarantee cards (single women, PwD, elderly 60+, released bonded labour, transgender persons) — priority worksites.

Must-know case Aruna Roy petition (SC, transition period): MGNREGA a "salutary scheme," not a freebie/exploitation — but Court questioned equating statutory work-guarantee with Art 21 fundamental right; no fundamental right to work recognized.
Must-know ratio Wage cost-sharing 60:40 (Centre:State, general states) / 90:10 (NE & Himalayan states) — the core "unfunded mandate" fiscal-federalism debate point.
  • 3 debates: (i) lean-season withdrawal → labour surplus → lower peak-season bargaining power; (ii) demand-driven realism vs. normative predictability/equity; (iii) centralization — unfunded mandate + wage-suppression risk (base rate may undercut state minimum wage; fix = CPI-indexation).
  • Way forward: GST-style transition compensation grant to states; manual-exception protocol for biometric failures; advance seasonal labour calendars via Gram Sabha; CPI-indexed base wage.
