1. AFSPA

  • Law (1958): s.3 declares "disturbed areas" (Centre or State); s.4 lethal force, warrantless arrest and search; s.5 hand over arrestees with least possible delay; s.6 prior sanction of the Centre needed to prosecute.
  • Constitutional basis: Entry 2A (Union List, 42nd Amendment); Art. 355.
  • Context: six-month extension from 1 Oct 2026 in parts of Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Concepts: state of exception and bare life (Agamben); negative vs. positive peace (Galtung); security federalism; concentric circles of security.
  • Concerns: wide powers; s.6 immunity; alienation; fragile gains; ethnic security dilemma; Centre–State disagreement.
  • Judgments: Naga People's Movement (1997/98) — upheld with safeguards, six-month review; EEVFAM (2016) — no absolute immunity.
  • Committees: Jeevan Reddy (2005) — repeal; Verma (2013) — remove sexual offences from immunity.
  • Northeast: Meghalaya, Tripura revoked; Nagaland continued (peace incomplete); Manipur reimposed (ethnic conflict); Arunachal (trans-boundary risk).
  • Way forward: conditions-based withdrawal; police capacity; periodic judicial review.
Must-know: AFSPA secures negative peace; positive peace needs justice and conflict transformation.

2. CSR & development financing

  • Definition: social activity for positive change; two dimensions — how a company earns (ethically) and how it spends (giving back).
  • Thinkers: Friedman (business of business is business); Freeman (stakeholder theory); Carroll (CSR pyramid: economic, legal, ethical, philanthropic); Elkington (triple bottom line: profit, people, planet); Gandhi (trusteeship).
  • California consensus: tech-led, scalable, measurement-driven philanthropy vs. patient institution-building.
  • Constitution: Arts. 38, 39(b), 39(c).
  • Law: s.135, Companies Act 2013 — ₹500 cr net worth / ₹1,000 cr turnover / ₹5 cr net profit; 2% of average net profit (3 years); CSR Committee; Schedule VII.
  • Data (FY25): ~₹40,794 crore (record); education ~34%, health ~21%, environment ~7–9%; six states get ~60%; NE ~₹855 cr.
  • Phases: voluntary philanthropy → state-led model → mandatory (2013) → new models (SSE, blended finance).
  • Challenges: tax-like mandate; tax avoidance; crowding out; closed-loop foundations; regional disparity; greenwashing; political interference.
  • SSE: verified projects, zero-coupon zero-principal bonds (up to 10% of CSR), broad participation.
