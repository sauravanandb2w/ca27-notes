Freebies vs. Welfare

  • Freebies: unpaid goods/services, electorally motivated, unproductive (e.g. free bus travel, unconditional cash transfers to women)
  • Welfareism: constitutionally obligated (Art. 38, 39(a)/(b)/(c), 41), capacity-building, long-term
  • New welfareism: state provision of private goods (house, toilet) — not itself criticized, only wasteful/unconditional spending is
  • Sanskrit lens: preyas (immediate pleasure) vs. shreyas (long-term good) — Katha Upanishad
  • Legal loophole: RPA 1951 §123 does not classify manifesto freebies as a "corrupt practice" (confirmed in Subramaniam Balaji v. TN, 2013)
  • Fiscal guardrail: state FRLs cap fiscal deficit at 3% GSDP — freebie spend pushes states toward this cap
  • Vicious cycle: freebies ↑ → taxes on paying/middle class ↑ → purchasing power ↓ → demand ↓ → tax revenue ↓ → more fiscal extraction
  • Other harms: capex crowd-out, resource distortion (free power → groundwater over-extraction), passive citizenry, intergenerational debt burden
  • Fixes: sunset clauses, periodic review, CCT models (Mexico's Prospera, Brazil's Bolsa Família), outcome-linked incentives, stricter EC manifesto disclosure norms

Must-know case law

S. Subramaniam Balaji v. Govt. of Tamil Nadu (2013): SC held manifesto freebie promises are not a "corrupt practice" under RPA 1951 §123; directed ECI to frame manifesto guidelines under the MCC.

Minimum Support Price (MSP)

  • MSP: pre-sowing floor price for 22 crops; CACP recommends → CCEA approves; purely executive, no statutory backing
  • A2+FL (govt.'s formula: input cost + family labour, +50%) vs. C2+50% (Swaminathan Commission: adds land rent + capital interest — farmers' demand)
  • FRP (sugarcane only) is legally binding under Sugarcane Control Order, 1966 — unlike MSP
  • Open-ended procurement (FCI buys unlimited wheat/rice) vs. Price Deficiency Payment (PDP) — govt. pays MSP–market gap, no procurement needed
  • WTO AoA: MSP = Amber box (trade-distorting); 10% de minimis cap for developing countries; Bali Peace Clause (temporary) shields India — India wants it permanent
  • Dual objective dilemma: protect farmer income vs. protect consumers from inflation — governments usually favour consumers near elections (export bans, ECA stock limits)
  • Tragedy of the commons: free power → groundwater over-extraction → aquifer depletion → deeper borewells → worse subsidy burden
  • Engel's Law: rising incomes shift diets from cereals to pulses/protein/fruit — but MSP still incentivizes cereal-centric output → India imports pulses/edible oil despite being a top rice/wheat producer
  • Legalization models: mandatory procurement / legal floor-price ban / statutory PDP — risk: trader-farmer collusion (MP), fiscal "domino effect" to all 22 crops
  • Global lessons: China (2014-16 decoupling — per-hectare payments, not price/output-linked); US PLC (statutory reference price, no procurement); both avoid open-ended procurement
  • PM-AASHA: PSS (physical procurement) + PDPS (deficiency payment) + PPSS (private procurement)
  • Way forward: index MSP to real-time input costs, shift to PDP, cap procurement (~40% for PDS needs), crop-neutral PDS sourcing, per-hectare income support

Must-know data (verified figures)

Food subsidy (2025-26 BE): ₹2.11 lakh crore; economic cost of rice: ₹41.73/kg; pulses import bill: ~$5.5 bn (2024-25); UCT burden: ₹1.7 lakh crore (Economic Survey 2025-26); 16th FC: state UCTs grew ₹73,099 cr (2018-19) → ₹4.14 lakh cr (2025-26).
