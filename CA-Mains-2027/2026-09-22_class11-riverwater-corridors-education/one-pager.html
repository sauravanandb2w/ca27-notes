1. Interstate River Water Disputes

  • Concepts: hydro-federalism; territorial trap (upper riparian treats water as absolute property); asymmetric riparian hydropolitics; Harmon Doctrine (absolute territorial sovereignty, favours upper riparian); equitable & reasonable utilization (incl. e-flow, not just arithmetic division); parchment vs practical rights; public trust doctrine.
  • Constitution: Art. 262(1) — Parliament legislates adjudication; Art. 262(2) — can exclude SC/court jurisdiction, but states still reach SC via Art. 131/136 in practice. State List Entry 17 (water supply/irrigation) is subject to Union List Entry 56 (interstate river regulation) — the "dual system."
  • IRWD Act 1956: state-triggered only; mandates a fresh ad hoc tribunal per dispute (no transfer to existing tribunal) — main cause of delay. River Boards Act 1956: dormant, unused.
  • Three ambiguities (go-to framework): (1) historical/geographical — colonial + linguistic boundaries ignored river basins; (2) constitutional/legal — Entry 17 vs 56; (3) institutional — tribunals not truly final due to Art. 131/136.
  • 2019 Amendment Bill: two-tier — DRC (1 yr, +6 mo) → single permanent tribunal, multiple benches (2 yr, +1 yr); dissolves all existing ad hoc tribunals.
  • Committees: Sarkaria — suo motu tribunal power, 1-yr constitution deadline, river basin orgs. Mihir Shah Committee — National Water Commission (merge CWC + CGWB).
  • Case studies: Kishau Multipurpose Project MoA (Sep 2026, 6 Yamuna-basin states, ended 17-yr deadlock) — cooperative-federalism success. International: Whanganui River legal personhood (NZ); Germany's LAWA; Mekong River Commission (shared data + joint EIA).
  • Way forward: new National Water Policy (current one is 2002); proactive Centre; Inter-State Council (Art. 263); river-basin-scale management; political consensus over litigation (MGR-Hegde anecdote).
Must-know: Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal — constituted 1990, Final Award 2007, notified 2013, SC final verdict 2018, CWMA set up 2018 (~27-28 yr span tribunal-to-resolution).
Case law/data: India has ~20% of world population but only ~4% of freshwater; per capita availability down from ~5,000 m³/yr (1950s) to ~1,486 m³/yr now; NITI CWMI (2018) — ~600 million Indians face high-extreme water stress.

2. Integrated Industrial Corridors / DFC

  • Concepts: platform infrastructure (planned together, e.g. PM GatiShakti) vs isolated infrastructure; New Economic Geography (Krugman) — lower transport cost → clustering, fixes spatial mismatch; growth pole (anchor industry, e.g. Manesar/Maruti); network externalities (utility rises exponentially per connected node); spatial equilibrium (equalizes factor costs, tier-2/3 cities become viable); industrial symbiosis.
  • China Plus One: MNC de-risking (not full decoupling) — India needs infra readiness to capture this shift.
  • DFC network: Eastern (Ludhiana–Sonnagar, Punjab to Bihar) + Western (UP to Maharashtra, JNPT-linked) = 2,843 km, fully operational (Sep 2026); ~430+ goods trains/day.
  • Logistics cost: India ~14-16% of GDP historically → now ~8% (matches developed-country benchmark). NCAER: rail ~Rs 1.96 vs road ~Rs 3.78 per unit distance. Modal share: India ~70% road/~27% rail vs China ~50% rail; National Rail Plan target: 45% rail share by 2030 (verified).
  • NICDP: 11 corridors, 13 states; employment estimate ~1-4 million (1.4m commonly cited) — treat the sometimes-quoted "100 million" figure as likely conflated with the unrelated National Manufacturing Policy 2011 target, not NICDP-specific.
  • Significance: competitiveness → investment → jobs; growth pole effect; GVC integration; decongestion; decarbonization (rail < road carbon footprint); maritime resilience (reduce dependence on Colombo/Singapore/Dubai transshipment via deep-draft ports like Vizhinjam); competitive federalism (e.g. Telangana vs AP).
  • Challenges: land acquisition + EIA/SIA "green hurdle"; fragmented multimodal regulation (CAG wants single regulator); freight-passenger cross-subsidization; first/last-mile gaps; high capital + long gestation; social/ecological conflict.
  • Way forward: land pooling (AP model); rationalize freight tariffs; NMP for private participation; last-mile feeders; fast-track East Coast & North-South DFCs; dedicated multimodal regulator.
Must-know: Logistics cost 14-16% → ~8% of GDP (do not confuse with the 45% rail-share target or EU's 30% road-to-rail-by-2030 shift target — three different percentages).
Case study: Maruti Suzuki in-plant rail siding (Gujarat) — rail dispatch share up from ~5% (2014) to ~26% (2025-26); World Bank LPI rank: 54 (2014) → 38 (2023).
Eastern DFC: Ludhiana → Sonnagar
Western DFC: UP → JNPT (Maharashtra)
FIRE = Freight corridor + Industrial corridor + Railways + Expressways → reduces friction + crowds in private capital

3. Education in the Concurrent List

  • Constitutional basis: Entry 25, List III (moved from State List Entry 11 by 42nd CAA 1976, along with forests, wildlife, weights & measures, administration of justice). Entry 25 is "subject to" Union List Entries 63-66; Entry 66 = Parliament's exclusive power over "coordination and determination of standards" in higher education/research — the anchor for UGC/NEET.
  • Case law: Gujarat University v. Krishna Ranganath Mudholkar — Entry 66 prevails over state education power in conflict, includes power to prevent dilution of standards. CMC Vellore Assn. v. UOI — NEET upheld under Entry 66; minority institutions can select only among NEET-qualified candidates.
  • Current friction: NEP 2020 three-language formula opposed by Tamil Nadu; "tied" funding (Samagra Shiksha, PM SHRI linked to full NEP compliance) = "coercive federalism" criticism; recent SC case: states can't act like separate countries, urged cooperative dialogue.
  • Arguments for Concurrent List: uniform quality standards; national integration/mobility (ABC, NCrF, JNVs, KVs); skilling aligned to Industry 4.0; regional-inequality correction (Samagra Shiksha, PM SHRI); insulates HEIs from local politics (UGC).
  • Counter-arguments: states bear ~75% of spending burden but limited say; cognitive-load criticism of mandatory 3rd language; unequal burden (southern states face a genuinely new language vs northern states via Hindi/English/Sanskrit).
  • "Laboratories of federalism": Tamil Nadu mid-day meal (scaled nationally); AP's Mana Badi: Nadu-Nedu; Odisha's multilingual L1→L2→L3 tribal/PVTG program (vs Central Board's rigid Class-6 introduction).
  • Committees: Kothari Commission (origin of 3-language formula); NCRWC (political neutrality in VC/Chancellor appointments); Justice Kurian Joseph Committee (TN govt, on state autonomy — interim report due Jan 2026, full report 2028; treat "recommended transfer back to State List" as TN's demand, not a completed finding); Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education (de-link scheme funds from conditionalities).
  • Way forward: revive CABE; permanent Inter-State Education Council; de-link scheme funding from rigid conditionalities.
Must-know: Union Education Ministry's Union Budget share fell ~4.6% (2013-14) → ~2.5% (2025-26); states bear ~75% of school-education spending; ~94,000 govt schools closed/consolidated in a decade; India runs ~5x as many schools as China.
Data point: ~36% of Indian schools have fewer than 50 students (verified: UDISE+ <10 + 11-20 + 21-30 + 31-50 bands sum to ~36%) — core evidence for the school-rationalization/quality-vs-access debate (Amartya Sen's "quantity over quality" counter-view).
